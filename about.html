About Me

Who I Am

I didn’t start out set on Computer Engineering — I was originally drawn to more hands-on fields like architecture and traditional engineering. I chose Computer Engineering for its flexibility, but at first, the concepts felt abstract and disconnected from real-world impact. That changed during a project-based lab where I built my first IoT system — an automated plant watering device using Arduino, a soil moisture sensor, a relay, and the Blynk app. That was the first time I saw hardware and software working together to solve a real problem, and it completely changed how I approached engineering. From there, I became more intentional about learning through projects. I explored Python, SQL, web development, and C/C++, with C++ and low-level programming becoming areas I especially enjoy for their structure, control, and connection to hardware. Over time, I developed a strong interest in embedded systems, firmware development, and understanding how hardware and software interact at a deeper level. I’m also interested in how intelligent systems can enhance real-world engineering applications. I enjoy debugging and problem-solving — not just getting things to work, but understanding why they fail. I’ve learned that stepping back, analyzing problems, and iterating are often just as important as writing code. Right now, I’m focused on developing my skills in embedded systems, firmware, and low-level software while continuing to explore how intelligent systems can be integrated into engineering applications. For me, engineering is about creating systems that don’t just exist in code — but work in the real world.
